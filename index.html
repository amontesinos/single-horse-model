THE ARGUMENT

Start with your hand.

The key change: horses are intersections with our world. We are looking at slices of the creature, not its cast shadow.

01 / A WORLD AS THIN AS PAPER

Five fingers. Five separate circles.

Imagine pushing your fingers through an idealized sheet of paper. Where each finger meets it, the flat world contains a roughly circular cross-section. Its inhabitants cannot see the hand connecting those pieces outside their plane.

02 / NOW ADD ONE DIMENSION

Our universe is the “paper.”

Replace the 3D hand with a 4D octopus-horse creature. Replace 2D paper with our 3D world. Each tentacle is shaped so its intersection with our world is a whole, solid horse—with length, height, and depth.

03 / SCALE UP THE ABSURDITY

Approximately sixty million tentacles.

In this conjecture, every horse on Earth is one of those intersections. The creature stays connected along a spatial direction we cannot point to. Separate horses here do not require separate creatures there.

Rounded scale, not a live census. The UN cites about 60.8 million horses in FAO’s 2023 data.

OBJECTION: “WHY DON’T THEY ALL ACT THE SAME?”

One being.
Many questionable decisions.

Borrow the octopus’s distributed nervous system. Its arms contain extensive neural circuitry, and its suckers can detect chemicals by contact—often described as tasting by touch. Arms can handle some sensing and movement locally, while the central brain still coordinates behavior.

The speculative leap: each horse-shaped branch has enough local control to explore, react, and pursue its own deeply unserious agenda. One shared parent need not mean synchronized behavior.

“Each horse cross-section is able to do whatever goofy shit it wants.”

Octopus arm control inspires the analogy. It does not establish a shared nervous system between actual horses.

OBJECTION: “THEN WHY ARE HORSES DIFFERENT?”

Five fingers.
Five fingerprints. One hand.

Even the fingers of one hand carry different ridge patterns. Being parts of one larger structure does not require being identical.

Your conjecture’s answer: different tentacles have different local structure, expressed in our slice as different horses. The lab gives each sample a different build, coat, and movement pattern.

Fingerprints are an analogy for variation within a whole. Different fingerprints are not evidence of different genomes, and do not explain horse genetics.

won’t fit in your head.

A flat-world inhabitant could catalogue every finger-circle and still miss the hand. We might catalogue every horse and still miss the hypothetical creature connecting them.

We can describe a fourth spatial coordinate mathematically without being able to see it directly. The tentacles in this diagram are a way to show connections; they are not a literal portrait of a 4D animal.

The geometry, the biology, and where the conjecture begins

This is a constructed geometric thought experiment, not evidence that real horses are one organism. The hand analogy demonstrates how a connected object can have separate cross-sections. It does not establish a biological or physical connection between horses.

A cross-section is not a projection. A projection maps an entire object into fewer dimensions. A cross-section includes only the points that lie in the selected plane or hyperplane. The new lab computes branch cross-sections at one chosen w value. It does not drop w from all the creature’s points.

The branches join outside the ordinary herd slice.

What this demo constructs: six continuous 4D branches whose 3D profiles grow from tips, remain horse-shaped, and converge into one common horse-shaped core. At the shared-body endpoint every branch has the same position, geometry, and orientation, so the union is connected there. In the middle, the six profiles occupy separate locations. A continuous connected 4D solid can therefore yield disconnected 3D sections. These six representatives stand in for the proposed millions; they are not an anatomical model of a real creature.

“Animate the herd” varies ordinary animation time while leaving the selected w slice fixed. The fourth coordinate is an extra spatial direction, not time. The left drawing compresses those connections into a schematic; the right view shows the actual 3D profiles generated by the model. The floor grid helps show depth and is not the whole 3D universe.

The hand mode uses five idealized rounded finger branches and a common rounded palm region. Finger sections are only approximately circular in real anatomy. A truly flat inhabitant would view the boundaries from within the plane, rather than see the overhead diagram we show.

Independent behavior: octopus arms have substantial peripheral neural processing and local motor capacity, but central control also matters. They are not eight fully independent brains. The proposal that each horse has its own local autonomy is an added premise, not a result of octopus research.

Genetic differences: ridge-pattern differences among fingers do not imply different genomes. Real horse genetic variation involves inheritance and mutation. The fingerprint comparison supplies an image of different parts belonging to one whole; a 4D biological explanation would need its own evidence and testable predictions.

The population scale: approximately 60 million is rounded from the UN’s report of 60.8 million horses in FAO’s 2023 data. It is not a current live total or a measured tentacle count.
